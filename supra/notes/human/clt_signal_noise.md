# CLT signal noise

> Digest of [clt_signal_noise.md](../clt_signal_noise.md) — the dense note is canonical; if they disagree, it wins.

**Update 2026-09-29 — the rewire worked.** Hot re-log after moving CLT and IAT to sensor ground: `CLT Voltage` noise 1.58 → 0.12 quanta, p99.9 0.177 → 0.020 V, no spikes; `IAT Voltage` 1.65 → 0.15 quanta. What is left is 1-LSB dither. The `cltTbl` dead band is **not** fixed (`CLT` sat at exactly 96.0 for 10.6 min), and oil-pressure jitter is unchanged. Details: [log_2026-09-29_allchannels_smoothclt.md](log_2026-09-29_allchannels_smoothclt.md) §4. (2026-09-28: Will confirmed CLT and IAT had been on block ground.)

**What this covers:** where the coolant-temp noise in the 2026-09-19 `fullchannels.csv` comes from, what the manual says, and whether the fix is the pull-up, the wiring, or the calibration.

**The rules:**

- The noise is electrical and engine-made. Key-on/engine-off the voltage is dead flat for 30 s; it starts dithering on the first firing sample and never stops. That clears the ECU, the 5 V rail, and the sensor element.
- All of the error is in the voltage. Re-running the tune's own calibration against the logged voltage reproduces the logged `CLT` to 0.50 °C mean error — but only after allowing for the **one-sample (40 ms) lag** between `CLT Voltage` and `CLT`. Compare them on the same row and the relationship looks inverted.
- It is not the fan. The 191 hot-and-fan-off samples are statistically identical to the 32 748 hot-and-fan-on ones.
- **ECUMaster's diagram shows both sensors two-terminal, both returns tied to B29 Sensor GND** (CLT signal B5, IAT B32). Nothing goes to engine or chassis ground. Wired to spec, CLT and IAT would share one return.
- **They don't behave like they share one.** Measured on the raw voltage channels (`IAT Voltage`, not the 1 °C-quantized `IAT`), spike-coincidence lift is **1.03×** and Pearson r is **0.073** — independent to within measurement error. Meanwhile `Analog 5` (= fuel pressure, a 3-wire sensor on the ECU's own sensor ground) is quieter than the log can resolve. The two temp sensors are referenced to two different things.
- **CLT and IAT are not the same fault.** CLT spikes are 71 % single-sample and **70 % toward +5 V**; IAT spikes are **99 % single-sample and 82 % toward ground**, with a 1.8× smaller tail (≈ 1.8 °C, tolerable). Rewire IAT while you're in there, but don't treat it as corroboration — CLT is the outlier.
- Independence is **evidence for local engine grounding, not against it.** Engine ground is a distributed conductor, not a node; two sensors bonded to the block at different points glitch independently. It only rules out a common bouncing *node*.
- The coupling is a **series disturbance in the sensor leg**. Noise is ~2.8× worse hot at matched RPM and load; capacitive pickup and 5 V-rail noise both predict ~3× worse *cold*, so they are ruled out by direction.
- **Dwell does not vary with RPM.** corr(dwell, RPM) = −0.004; corr(dwell, battery voltage) = −0.986; partial corr controlling for vbat = −0.075. With vbat held at 13.6–13.9 V dwell is 4.15 ms flat from 1000–3200 rpm. It looks RPM-dependent only because vbat rises at cruise. `Overdwell` never sets.
- **Coil-vs-injector is unresolved.** The earlier "constant per spark" argument was pooled across the log and does not survive the time drift below. Coil current is still the leading candidate on physical grounds, but this log does not prove it.
- **The fault worsens with heat soak.** Two effects: the §1f temperature term (2.7× across one six-minute warm-up ramp at steady RPM — which is how the divider argument survives the confound), *plus* a slower drift where, at matched coolant temperature, bulk dither rises only 1.26× but **spike rate rises 5.3×** over the drive. Impulses get more frequent as things expand — a thermally marginal connection, not a fixed coupling geometry. Oil pressure drifts the opposite way.
- **One `cltTbl` cell is broken** and does most of the visible damage. Fit Steinhart-Hart to the table's own 14 points: drop the 0.588 V cell and the other 13 fit to 0.32 °C RMS; drop any other and the fit falls apart. That cell duplicates its neighbour and reads 8.6 °C hot.
- The pull-up is a **bool on a fixed 2K2**, not a selector; changing it means an external resistor plus a regenerated table. A pull-*down* does not apply to an NTC. The manual offers it for sensor-range matching, never as a noise remedy.
- **A continuity beep from the CLT ground wire to the block proves nothing with the ECU plugged in** — sensor ground (pins 29/38/39) reaches the block through the ECU's own ground (17/24/27/28). Unplug the ECU, then meter harness-side CLT ground to B29 and to the block. The sensor is **two-pin** (Will, 2026-09-25), so a 1-wire sender is ruled out; also check its ground pin to its own body reads open.
- Order: fix the cal cell → count the sensor's pins and measure the offset → fix the wiring → re-log → *then* consider the pull-up.

**Key numbers:** dead band 0.451–0.588 V all reads 96 °C — **65 % of hot running time**; slope at its edge −89 °C/V, so a 0.2 V glitch = 18 °C. `CLT` excursions p99 = 6 °C, p99.9 = 13 °C, max 26 °C. Predicted offset at the sensor, engine running: **100–350 mV impulsive** — measure it between the sensor's ground terminal and B29 to confirm or kill this diagnosis. 68 % of spikes toward colder. Spike rate 0.00 % in the first three minutes → 4–9 % late in a 32-minute drive — **backprobe hot**. Bad cell should read **87 °C**. Sensor at 90 °C ≈ 270 Ω against 2200 Ω — 15.7 mV/°C; a 1K external gives 27.1 mV/°C.

**Inventory (all 564 channels scored on residual-vs-1s-median, normalized by each channel's quantum, plus a sign-reversal rate to separate noise from fast real signal):** the only genuinely noisy raw inputs are **CLT Voltage**, **IAT Voltage** (milder, opposite sign) and **CAN Analog 5 = engine oil pressure** — which actually has the worst absolute jitter in the log (σ 0.22 bar, p99.9 1.6 bar on a 4.1 bar median) and is a *separate* unresolved problem on the Switchboard, not correlated with CLT. It is confirmed on sensor ground with a short run, and despite the coils sitting close to that run the noise is **not coil-synchronous** — its rate is flat in time and falls 3× per spark from idle to 3000 rpm, i.e. continuous broadband noise, not impulses. `Mux switch voltage raw` is a floating unused input — ignore. `Battery voltage` is alternator ripple. `Internal MAP voltage` and `TPS voltage` move because the engine and throttle move. `Analog 5` (fuel pressure), `PPS Voltage` and the other CAN analogs are clean. Knock voltage channels are supposed to be noisy.

**When to care:** any time CLT jumps more than a couple of degrees between samples, and before blaming idle, warmup enrichment, ASE, or fan behaviour on calibration. After the rewire the glitches did not become correlated — there is nothing left to correlate (residual r 0.05, no spikes); that is the cleaner outcome.
