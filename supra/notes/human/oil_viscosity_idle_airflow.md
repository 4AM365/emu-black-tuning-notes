**What this covers:** how much extra idle airflow the engine needs when the coolant is at 96 °C but the oil is still cold, measured off 11 logs from May 24 – July 30, 2026, and **independently re-verified 2026-08-01 across a broader 16-log set** — both anchors reproduced.

**The rules:**

- **The two anchors.** Hot coolant + **cold** oil needs **51–58 airflow % (5.2–5.6 % TPS)**. Hot coolant + **hot** oil needs **28–31 % (3.9–4.1 % TPS)**. The adder is **+20 to +29 airflow %**, call it **+25 (≈ +1.4 % TPS)** — the engine wants roughly **1.8×** its hot-oil idle airflow when the oil is cold.
- **Both anchors lean the same way, so the real gap is wider, not narrower.** Where the cold number came from a log with the old +15 PID clamp, the loop was pinned *at* the clamp with RPM sagging 80 below target — it wanted more than it got. Where the hot number came from, the PID was pinned at its *negative* clamp — the engine was getting more than it needed.
- **The cleanest single measurement is `before_increased_active_airflow.csv` (July 30)** — the only cold start logged after the clamp went 15 → 25. Demand peaked at **58 % airflow / 5.6 % TPS with the PID at +18.8 of 25 (not clipped)**, then fell ~19 points in the next two minutes.
- **Demand peaks 1.5–3 minutes *after* coolant reaches 96 °C**, not at the crossing, and doesn't settle until **8–12 min after that** (11–15 min total runtime). That rise-then-fall is exactly the "runs fine, then struggles, then recovers" symptom.
- **It really is the oil.** Oil pressure normalized for pump speed keeps falling **44 %** after coolant is flat at 96 °C. Charge temp *rises* 40 → 52 °C over the same window, which would demand *more* throttle — so charge heating can't explain the drop. Friction can.
- **Viscosity law: shape matches, magnitude doesn't — so don't scale the adder by oil pressure.** Heywood: friction ~**doubles** from warm (90–100 °C) to just-started (20 °C), while bulk viscosity rises ~**10–13×** (most FMEP is viscosity-insensitive; friction ∝ μ^~0.3). The measured airflow swing hot→cold-oil is **1.9× (29 → 56 %)** — right on the friction curve, not the viscosity curve. A raw viscosity-index fit (R² 0.45) extrapolates to a nonsensical >100 % airflow at 30 °C. **The adder saturates near +27–30 airflow % (≈2× baseline) and climbs no further.**
- **Oil temp bins (1200 rpm), friction-shaped:** 30 °C **+28**, 49 °C **+27**, 68 °C **+24**, 86 °C **+13**, 105 °C **0**. 105/86 measured; 49/30 sit at the ceiling and are cold-start extrapolation (oil is never that cold under hot coolant). Cooler diverts (not blocks) above ~82 °C → idle oil regulates ~90–105 °C, so no bin above 105 is needed.
- **Better still — index on OIL PRESSURE, not oil temp (Result 5).** `P ∝ μ·N` and friction `∝ μ·N`, the same product, so one pressure axis folds temperature *and* rpm into the friction quantity. Validated: `air = 4.13·P + 22.5`, R² 0.51; adding rpm lifts R² by 0.002 and the **viscosity index fits *worse*** — raw pressure is the right axis (friction ∝ μ·N = P). **Fitted correction = clip(4.13·(P − P_hot(N)), 0, 27)**, with hot-idle pressure `P_hot = 1.885e-3·N` (2.26 bar @1200; your 2.4 is in-scatter). AGM absorption wants **14.4–14.8 V**; this alternator peaks at 14.00 V cold and settles to **13.40 V hot** — below even a proper AGM float (13.6–13.8). The car can't charge it past a partial state, which is why the periodic AGM-charger top-up is the compensation. **For idle tuning this cuts the other way from intuition: a low charge voltage limits accepted current, so it makes the recharge load *smaller*** — a third independent reason the alternator isn't the step.

**Key numbers:** cold-oil peak 51–58 airflow % / 5.2–5.6 % TPS · hot-oil floor 28–31 % / 3.9–4.1 % TPS · adder ≈ **+25 airflow %** · peak lands t(CLT 96) **+90–180 s** · settles t(CLT 96) **+8–12 min** · viscosity index (oil bar per 1000 rpm) falls 3.1 → 1.7 across that window with CLT flat · **demand ceiling reached by ~5 bar (R9), or ~3.3 bar (R12), not 8** · hot-oil 1200-rpm requirement **30.0 total airflow %** at 2.25 bar (R12) · idle loop gain **13–16 rpm per airflow %**.

**When to care:** building the oil-temp idle-airflow table, sizing the PID clamps, or judging whether a warm-idle stumble is a tune fault or the oil-lag window.
