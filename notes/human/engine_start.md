# Cranking and cold-start

> Digest of [engine_start.md](../engine_start.md) — the dense note is canonical; if they disagree, it wins.

**What this covers:** getting the engine lit and through the first 5–10 seconds — cranking airflow and enrichment, the handoff into the idle system, and why post-start stumbles are almost never fuel.

**The rules:**

- A post-start flare, sag, or stumble is an air/timing problem until proven otherwise. Do not reach for ASE — the restart runs open-loop (wideband blind ~25–33 s), so a "lean sag" can't even be measured during the event.
- `idleCrankingDC` is an airflow % (same unit as the active table; the "DC" name is historical) and it is **held open-loop through catch** — the idle PID stays gated for `idleControlAfterstartDelay`. Whatever you put in the hot bins is the airflow the engine flares on.
- **`crankingThreshold` means "the engine has started" — set it there, not where a cranking pulse can reach** (Will, 2026-09-29; Supra set to 750 with afterstart delay 4 — re-read the export). Place it above everything before the catch and below the lowest running stumble: 586–832 rpm in the 09-29 log. 1025 was rejected because hot idle dips below it and re-entry below the threshold is unverified. The afterstart delay's job is the run-up from the threshold to target: 0.39 s on the 09-29 cold start (→ 4 counts); re-measure per start type. At 500 the exit fired on a 586 rpm starter pulse, 0.5 s before the engine caught, and the idle PIDs engaged at 531 against 1677. Don't bend the afterstart delay or another parameter to cover an early exit. Once the threshold marks a real start, the cranking tables have to carry the engine up to it: air (now the run-up air), spark, and fuel. At the 09-29 exit, running fuel was ×1.36 of cranking fuel, so the cranking-fuel rev tail near the exit and ASE runtime-0 must meet. Stall is declared below `engineStallRevs`, a direct rpm (UI max 250; lowered below 60 on 09-29 so fuel starts sooner) that **must sit below the lowest cranking speed** (158–222 rpm on 09-29), or a normal crank reads as a stall. The 08-24 "keep it at 400" verdict is superseded; see the canonical note.
- **One cranking airflow can't be right for both outcomes — the plate has two stable branches.** Airflow % is a plate *position*: at ~400 rpm / MAP 83 it passes ~4.5 g/s (enough to idle there, not to climb); at ~1330 rpm / MAP 40 the same position passes ~8.4 g/s. Low RPM → high MAP → less mass → stays low. 37.5 % flared to 1728 on 06-13 and sagged to 386 on 08-24 for exactly this reason. Don't chase a perfect cranking airflow — give the PID authority sooner (`idleControlAfterstartDelay` down) so whichever branch you land on gets corrected before it sets. Note the base tables already match (`idleActiveAirflow` at the afterstart target ≈35.6 % vs 36–37.5 % cranking); the 36 → 62 % jump in the log was **PID output**, not a table step.
- Flare-fix lever order: hot cranking bins down (sets the level), afterstart delay down (held shorter), ignition lock time/restore down (frees the fast retard lever), then a small `idleAirFlowKD` for the residual swing. Kill the flare and the undershoot shrinks with it.
- **Exit-to-target time depends on temperature** (2026-10-02): ≈ 1.5 s cold (hover), ≈ 0.1–0.25 s warm/hot, with the warm flare peak at ≈ 0.65 s. A 1.5 s hold is clean cold. It engaged on the falling edge at CLT 82, where the held airflow was below the need (trough 615, overshoot 1430). Both hot dips had the A/C clutch engage mid-hold, so the hot case is untested. Delays of ≈ 0.4–1.2 s land at the flare peak.
- Set each cranking CLT bin to the active-airflow % at the RPM you actually catch into — idle target **plus** the afterstart bump — with a small margin so the first correction is a gentle pull-down. That makes the 400 rpm handoff stepless.
- The post-start throttle slam (cranking TPS → lower idle TPS) creates an air deficit ASE cannot cover. Fix it with an elevated idle target for 5–10 s after start, not more fuel.
- Cranking enrichment pays the wall-film tax, not air starvation — big positive cold, small hot. The decay per rev is the wall-film (Aquino x-τ) model: the film fills per intake event, which is **why revolutions is the correct axis, not time**. Shape ≈ exponential from the rev-1 peak; colder = higher peak + longer decay, hotter = lower peak + faster collapse (at 112 °F, warm cells should be ~0 by rev 7–13). Ethanol clears slower cold, so `crankingCorrTbl2` decaying slower than Tbl1 cold is correct.
- Don't target a cranking MAP or chase cranking vacuum. Cranking sits near atmospheric by physics (the engine can't pull the manifold down at ~180 rpm; a cam raises it further), and the literature agrees — Bosch/Bell/Hartman compensate poor cold vaporization with **fuel quantity**, not manifold pressure. The throttle here can't get below ~88 kPa anyway without sacrificing the idle floor. Enrich sensibly (bounded by plug-wetting); vacuum is a held-in-reserve option (~78 kPa), not the fix.
- On a cammed engine the dominant cranking penalty is **low compression from late intake-valve closing** (BC0311: cranking DCR ~8.2 vs static 10), not overlap. VVT can't advance to help — no oil pressure at cranking — so you always crank at the low-compression position. It wants a healthy shot of fuel and maybe a couple extra degrees of cranking timing; faster cranking (battery/starter/grounds) buys compression and spark energy at once.
- Cranking fuel = main-VE-based dose × cranking table (CLT × crank-revs) × anti-flood pedal scale × √ΔP fuel-pressure comp. **Never crank with your foot in it** — any pedal cuts the dose, ≥90% cuts all of it (that's the clear-flood mode). The √ΔP comp clamps at 2.39×, so an empty rail = no fuel regardless of PW. The anti-flood scale reads **TPS**, so on DBW it also cuts at the idle controller's cranking throttle position with no pedal at all (Supra 10-02: −16 % cold, −9 % hot) — keep it at 0 up to just above the highest cranking TPS.
- **The cranking % rides on the VE table, so a "0 %" cell is not neutral.** v3 doses cranking through the main VE equation, and the lookup clamps to the lowest-RPM row — the one row a wideband can never autotune. If true trapped VE is below what that row claims (late IVC + no ram at ~180 rpm, plus hot-restart charge heating the IAT sensor misses), every cell is silently enriched by the ratio. Measure it (B = VE_true / VE_table) before reading any cranking percentage as literal. On this build B ≈ 0.79, so 0 % was delivering λ ≈ 0.79. Option: re-scale `rpmBins` to start at ~200 rpm instead of 500 (table size is fixed; the axis is editable), with B × the 500 rpm VE there, and lift the cranking table ×1/B. The speed error then fades with RPM on its own (the bog at 455–586 shifts only −3…0 %), and the cranking table keeps only the wall-film decay. Without the ×1/B lift, B is counted twice and cranking runs ~21 % lean. **Rejected:** the 500 rpm bin is Will's bog-rescue row (it works). Re-scaling the axis down would blend the bog with a cranking bin (≈ −8 % at 455 rpm), so B stays in the cranking table.
- The cranking correction tables **do accept negative cells** despite their `u12` storage name (EMU writes them sign+magnitude). Put the basis correction there, not in `crankingLambdaTarget` — that would work but disguises a calibration error as a lean target.
- When rebuilding the tables, **do not back-solve the evaporated fraction from the old working tables** — the basis term cancels and you reproduce the tables you were trying to replace. Pick the wall-film parameters from physics and say they're picks.
- **With the threshold at 'started', the cranking fuel tail fuels a running engine.** The late rev rows used to apply only to a crank that wasn't catching. Now they carry the hover and run-up (on 09-29 the engine turned another 4.5 revs between the old 500 exit and 750 rpm; in the ECU's rev count that is ≈14.5, or ≈18 if the counter runs ~1.8× physical as the log suggests). Their negative values are the cranking-speed VE correction, which no longer applies once the VE lookup is on real rows at 500–1025 rpm. Size the tail so the cranking dose at the exit meets the running dose (ASE runtime-0): about +40 % at CLT 26 from the 09-29 exit (×1.36). Or bring ASE down to meet it; the WBO can't yet say which. Proposed shape: keep rows 1/3/7 (cranking speed), set the exit row (13 for a cold 750 exit) = ASE runtime-0, and keep row 20 low as anti-flood for a crank that isn't catching.
- The crank-revolutions axis counts even when no fuel is flowing. Cranking on a filling rail burns the rich rows into an unfueled engine; there is no usable start-delay setting in the software, so the fix is making the key-on pump prime actually run (rail full at rev 1).
- **Prime pulse** (one-shot injection at first sync, all injectors) front-loads the rev-1 wall film so the first spark has vapor → faster catch. Size it aggressive **cold**, **zero hot** (extra hot liquid fouls the cold plugs), and **cut the early cranking revs by the same fuel you add** or you double-dose and flood. Full method + suggested table: [prime_pulse.md](../prime_pulse.md).

- **Injected ≠ measured during warm-up.** The warm-up enrichment (+~23 % effective at E60, CLT ~32 °C) feeds the port wall films; the wideband only sees the burned gas, and the tune's λ target during warm-up is deliberately unchanged (`warmupTblLambdaCorrTbl` = 0). Correct warm-up reads **near target with a mild rich bias** (film burn-off + cold HC breakthrough) after the ~30 s blind window. **Sustained severe rich (λ ≤ ~0.85 for minutes) is over-fueling** — bore wash and plug fouling, not a cold-engine requirement.
- **Rich reading + lean stumble when pulled = don't pull uniformly.** Ranked causes: cold-amplified per-cylinder spread (average rich, leanest cylinder at the misfire edge — check per-cyl EGT spread cold vs warm); E60 preferential evaporation (ethanol skips the early vapor, pays back as a mid-warm-up rich hump → fix the CLT *schedule*, not the level); and a legitimately-rich cold burned-mixture need that should be **commanded** via the zeroed warm-up λ-target table instead of smuggled in as surplus.

**Key numbers:** `crankingThreshold` 750 + afterstart delay 4 on the Supra 2026-09-29 (re-read the export; VE axis floor 500 rpm); `engineStallRevs` below cranking speed (158–222 rpm on 09-29); `Idle state` on this build **3 = cranking, 5 = afterstart delay, 2 = active**; measured catch ≈ **2 crank revolutions / 0.5 s** warm, then ~25 s to settle; afterstart delay 5 ≈ 480 ms open-loop; ignition lock ×0.04 s/count; wideband blind ~25–33 s. Cranking timing 10° BTDC — conservative; the kickback ceiling is set by *cranking* DCR (~8.2 here → behaves like 8:1, tolerates ~20°), **not** static CR, so A-B toward 12–15° and watch for kickback (labored crank/buck); dwell 6.2 ms at 10 V (D585 LS coils fully saturated, no overdwell); cranking voltage ~10.4 V (workable, low side). Starting-timing condition table + afterstart lock/ramp are in the canonical note. Idle timing starting ranges: pump/stock 10–12°, pump/cammed 13–16°, ethanol/cammed 16–19°, ethanol/big cams 19–22° (base below MBT for reserve).

**When to care:** any start complaint — hard start, flare-then-sag, post-start stall — and whenever you set cranking airflow, ASE, or the afterstart ignition lock.
