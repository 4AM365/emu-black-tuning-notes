# Idle

> Digest of [idle.md](../idle.md) — the dense note is canonical; if they disagree, it wins.

**What this covers:** every EMU Black idle table — what it does, which direction to move it, and the two-PID architecture that decides which lever fixes which idle problem.

**The rules:**

- Two PIDs: ignition is the fast lever, airflow the slow one (it re-centers the ignition angle, not RPM). Knock down a high idle with retard, never an air purge — retard reverses in one cycle; a purge leaves the throttle shut for the next load step.
- That's **mid-ranging control**: spark is fast but narrow (~±5°), air is slow (~740 ms) but unbounded, so air chases spark back to `Target ignition angle` to keep advance *and* retard reserve. No airflow PID is needed to *run* — old engines idled on droop plus open-loop schedules (choke cam = CLT airflow table, A/C idle-up = fan comp). What the PID buys is symmetric ignition authority without scheduling every disturbance by hand; without it, base-table error parks the ignition PID off-center and one side of its reserve is gone.
- Idle runs ~30% residual gas; cams and turbo backpressure push it toward the misfire cliff. No PID authority fixes combustion instability — raise the target, run slightly rich, add base timing, nail per-cylinder trim first.
- Feed-forward corrections (`idleCustomCorrection`, fan/AC comp) get roughly **half** the airflow change the engine needs; the PID eases in the rest. Under-correcting idles briefly high; over-correcting stalls.
- The MAP activation gate (`idleMinMapToActivate`) reads backwards until you see that engine braking pulls *deeper* vacuum than idle. Idle activates when MAP is **above** the gate. Set it just above worst-case engine-braking MAP and well below the idle-MAP floor (check cold fast-idle too). Too high → return-to-idle stall.
- **`Open loop over VSS` is a decoupling gate, not a high-speed cutoff.** With the clutch/neutral escapes it says *close the idle loop only when crank speed is the throttle's responsibility.* Pedal up and rolling in gear, RPM is a driveline observable; the loop winds against an error it can't fix, and that posture lands on the engine when the clutch opens — **dip if it wound negative, flare if positive**, sign set by the last few seconds of grade and gear. Inconsistent clutch-in behaviour is the signature. So **set it low (single-digit km/h)**, not high — but only as low as clears the speed channel's standstill noise floor: the force **wipes both integrals**, and a glitch at a true stopped idle is a stall shape. Validate the speed signal first.
- `idleActiveAirflow` is indexed by idle **target**, which floors at the lowest axis bin — its sub-idle rows are never read. Raising them does nothing for a return-to-idle dip; the VE table owns that.
- The indexing target is the **ramp-inflated** one (`idleRPM` + remaining `idleRAMPDownOffset`), so `idleTargetBins` must span *setpoint + offset* or every idle **entry** clamps to the top row. **Re-check the axis whenever `idleRPM` or `idleRAMPDownOffset` changes** — it goes stale silently.
- **If the bottom bin sits above the commanded hot target, two operating points share one row** and it can't serve both: the lower runs a standing negative PID (benign surplus), the higher a standing positive one with the integral pinned. **Fix the axis, not the values** — trimming the shared cell helps one and worsens the other.
- **Re-siting `idleTargetBins` re-labels rows without moving the data.** Resample the old surface onto the new bins and re-enter every row, or each row inherits a calibration made for a different target.
- **Verify the axis, don't assume it.** Target and RPM are collinear across most of a closed-loop idle log, so a whole-log fit can't separate them. Compare lookups on target vs RPM using only samples where they differ by >150 rpm (catch transient, load sag) — on the Supra that gave residual sd 0.59 vs 4.29.
- `idleArmedAirFlow` (the overrun glide) must stay above minimum useful airflow and match the active value at the bottom bin. Too low at the decel bins → overrun-to-idle stall; the rich spike in the log is the symptom, not the cause.
- **`idleAfterstartRPMincrease` is post-start stall margin, not oil priming.** Its runtime axis is seconds, so it serves only seconds-long causes: wall film forming, friction before oil arrives (~45–55 revs from a soak), alternator recharge, and the cranking→loop handoff. Minutes-long cold effects belong to `idleRPM`. Size it to how close the engine sits to the stability limit at catch — cams need more, an inline-6's inertia needs less. OEM Toyota does a CLT warm-up schedule plus a start-up air opening, not a seconds-long flare; cat light-off is minutes-scale and needs a cat. Changing or zeroing it means re-deriving `idleCrankingDC` at the new landing target.
- `idleCrankingDC` is an airflow % (not a duty cycle) held open-loop through catch — it is the flare setpoint. Match it to the active-airflow value at target + afterstart bump, plus a small margin.
- Clamp the airflow-PID integral tighter than its total output, or windup returns — **but you can't clamp it below the steady-state correction the base table demands**, or the engine simply idles high by that much. A big base margin therefore forbids a tight integral clamp.
- **Before adding KD, measure its signal-to-noise.** The outer loop's error is the ignition correction, quantised at 0.5°, so one LSB per loop tick swamps the real transient: measured noise sd(dε/dt) ≈ 6–7 °/s against a desaturation signal of 1–3.6 °/s, SNR ≈ 0.3. A lag-compensating KD would inject ~18 rpm of continuous idle jitter to buy almost nothing. **Keep KD = 0 and buy the anticipation with kP.** Exception: fast load steps such as A/C engagement have SNR ≈ 10 (40–60 °/s peaks), so a small KD is a fair test there; verify with `Monitored D term`.
- **For trajectory following, shift authority from I to P.** P acts instantly, is bounded by the output clamp, and releases itself when the error clears — so it adds nothing to the in-flight overshoot. I is the term that must be unwound through the actuator lag, and that unwinding *is* the bog. Raising kP alongside KI cancels most of the overshoot the higher KI creates; there's a kP where `kP × rail` ≈ `KI × rail × lag` and the net goes to zero.
- **Setting `idleAirFlowKP` from a hunt:** regress `Idle air %` on `Ignition Angle` — the slope *is* kP in %/deg (the PID's error is ignition-angle error, and base airflow only moves the intercept). Napkin margin needs no amplitude, just cycles: `δ = ln(1/f)/n`, `ζ = δ/2π`, `kP/Ku ≈ 1 − δ/π`. Aim **0.25–0.30·Ku**, not ZN's 0.5·Ku — this is the *outer* loop of a mid-ranging pair and should be boring. Lower gain settles *faster*; residual idle "walking around" is the same P term amplifying ignition jitter (check `sd(air%)/sd(ign)` ≈ kP); KI needs no compensating change. Script: [emu-black-idle-pid-gain](../skills/emu-black-idle-pid-gain/SKILL.md).
- **Saturated ≠ slow.** If the airflow PID is pinned on `idleAirPIDOutMax`, that clamp is the *only* lever — gains and the update interval do nothing while it's on the rail. Size the clamp from the actuator mapping: throttle % per airflow % = (DBW target max − DBW Target min)/100.
- **The actuator range lives in *Idle → Actuator*** as **DBW Target min / DBW target max** — the same box that offers Min DC / Max DC for a solenoid. ECUMaster's setup text names that pair as the knob that stops the engine stalling at minimum airflow.
- **The two-step return to idle is inner-loop desaturation, not a gain fault.** RPM hangs high while the ignition PID sits on its min-torque-angle rail (the outer loop is then winding open-loop at a fixed rate and RPM barely moves), then drops in one step when the angle desaturates, then creeps in. **Gains change the durations; the step height is set by the retard depth.** The rail is unavoidable whenever base airflow exceeds what the engine needs by more than the ~10 airflow points the retard can mask.
- **A ramp-down offset that finishes before the engine does is a step in disguise.** If the target lands at idle while RPM is still hundreds high, the outer integral winds at its full railed rate for the whole lead time and can only stop late — that accumulated surplus is the undershoot/bog on arrival. Slow the decay rate until RPM falls *in sync* with the offset (ECUMaster's own instruction); the cost is a longer between-shift hang, which the same offset governs.
- **Two different ignition clamps — don't conflate them.** The ±5° integral limit bounds only the I component; the *total* output is clamped separately to the min/max **torque-angle tables** in absolute degrees. With kP ≈ 0.05 °/rpm, P alone hits the retard floor at ~140 rpm of error, so the integral never has to participate. Also: `Monitored P/I/D term` is the **airflow** PID's monitor, not the ignition PID's.
- **An over-aired active table is over-aired at the *held* target, not along the whole ramp.** Anchor the requirement at the measured held-idle airflow, never at the table's own cell. On the Supra the excess runs ~0 at the top of the ramp to ~24 points at the bottom — the ignition correction is ≈0 at the top and railed at the bottom, which is how you read it straight out of a log. Bin `Idle ignition correction` by commanded target; that curve *is* the excess profile.
- **Size the ramp-down offset so the crossover sits above where the ARMED table alone would settle the engine** — otherwise RPM stops falling and the controller never leaves ARMED. Measure it: bin ARMED samples (pedal up) by RPM and read the smoothed dN/dt; the band where it goes slack is the hang zone. Hard ceiling on the other side: `idleTargetBins` must span `idleRPM + offset`, or the base-airflow lookup silently clamps to the top row and the feed-forward stops responding to the ramp.
- **The ARMED glide and the ACTIVE approach are different animals.** ARMED falls fast because the armed table sits below equilibrium — a deliberate torque deficit. The active table's top row is *at* equilibrium, so the deficit vanishes at the handoff and deceleration stops. The loop then has to manufacture a new deficit by winding the integrator, which sets a hard floor on ramp duration: `excess / (KI × rail depth)`. Match the decay rate to that, or give ACTIVE a deficit to inherit.
- **Whether the ramp is trackable is arithmetic, not feel:** `D = KI × |ignition error| / (requirement slope − base-table slope)`. Measure the requirement slope by regressing `Idle air %` on RPM (and ignition) over the creep phase. If the base table's target-axis slope is much shallower than the requirement, the integrator is carrying feed-forward's job and no gain setting fixes that cleanly — re-sweep the upper rows with the ignition PID zeroed.
- **The ignition PID is not the lever for the step.** kP ≈ 0.05 °/rpm rails at ~140 rpm error, which is the right place; KI is deliberately glacial because the *airflow* loop owns the integral action in a mid-ranging pair. Only the min-torque-angle rail sets step height, and it trades step size against hang height and flare knock-down. Fix the saturation, not the inner loop.
- **The handoff knee is on the engine side.** Incoming deceleration collapses at ACTIVE entry with fuelling and timing unchanged — it's the airflow table switch. Matching the target's decay to the *arrival* slope has the sign backwards; it widens the gap to what the loop can deliver.
- **A fast decay rate is bought with feed-forward, never with gain.** The ramp trajectory is known in advance, so it's a feed-forward job; the integrator is a feedback device that must observe an error first, and its wind rate times the actuator lag is airflow already in flight when the inner loop desaturates — that's the bog. If a big protective margin sits in the base table, no table shape hides it inside a short ramp: it only relocates the hang from the middle to the entry. Shrink the margin (move it onto a correction axis that reads zero when it isn't needed) and the fast ramp tracks itself.
- **There is no exponential ramp shape** — `idleRAMPDownDecayRate` is rpm/s and `idleTargetRampDelay` is a pre-delay. The exponential approach comes from keeping the target's lead small enough that neither PID rails, so the closed loop's own time constant shapes the tail.
- **Check the ARMED → ACTIVE handoff for an air step.** The base source switches tables at target + offset; if the active table is taller there, the engine gets *more* air at the moment it should be decelerating.
- **`idlePIDUpdateInterval` is not a tunable — treat it as vestigial.** Absent from the Airflow PID dialog (which holds only 3 gains + 2 integral limits + 2 output clamps), undocumented across all 23 help pages, frozen at 200 in every export, orphaned in the XML beside starter/buzzer settings. **Measured loop rate is ~40 ms, not 200.** To check on any build: histogram the hold lengths of the PID-output channel in a log — a real 5 Hz loop shows 5-sample treads at 25 Hz. Gains are dt-normalized anyway (`%/°`, `%/(°·s)`, `%/(°/s)`), so an interval would scale no gain regardless.

**Key numbers:** cranking→active handoff at **400 rpm**; manifold time constant ~740 ms; DBW lag ~750 ms; engine braking ~13–17 kPa vs idle ~30–40 kPa; Airflow% ubyte tables decode ×0.5; **airflow-PID gains decode raw/1024** (2048 → 2 %/°).

**When to care:** any idle complaint — stall, hunt, hot drift, return-to-idle bog — and before touching any idle airflow table, PID gain, or the actuator range (rescale everything together).
